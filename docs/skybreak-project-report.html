02 / How it works

Three layers, with the server acting as referee

Client means the code running on the player's device: controls, camera, menus and effects. Server means the authoritative game simulation: enemy behavior, hits, mission progress and saving. Shared means rules and definitions both sides can read, so they agree on how the game behaves.

Evidence: src/server/index.server.ts · src/server/CombatService.ts · src/client/FlightController.ts · src/shared/FlightPrediction.ts · src/server/CampaignService.ts · src/server/CampaignRepository.ts

What happens during one missile shot?

1 / INPUTPlayer steers and presses FIRE.
2 / REQUESTClient sends controls and a combat action.
3 / REFEREEServer checks target, lock, ammo and cooldown.
4 / SIMULATIONServer advances missiles and resolves damage.
5 / FEEDBACKDevice draws effects; HUD and mission progress update.

The server simulates at 30 Hz. Authoritative flight snapshots are sent at 20 Hz and numeric HUD telemetry at 10 Hz. The client renders predicted motion between updates and corrects it toward server truth. Continuous streams use unreliable events; discrete actions and results use reliable events.

How the source becomes a Roblox game

TypeScript / TSX → roblox-ts compiler → Luau output → Rojo place mapping → Roblox Studio / published place. React Lua builds the interface. Blender/Python generators and packaged mesh data supply custom aircraft and mission assets, with fallback geometry paths. The HTML report you are reading is a project document, not part of the Roblox runtime.

04 / Quality priorities

Turn the current systems into a game people want to replay.

These are design judgments based on source and recorded evidence. We have not substituted code inspection for a human playtest.

1. Make audio demonstrably work

Verify recorded dialogue and custom effects in the official published experience on the affected phone. Capture actual audible output, correlate it with place version and permissions, then test pause, skip, volume and rejoin. Preserve existing recordings and investigate failures before producing more audio.

2. Tune flight around player intention

Loops and attitude hold already exist. Test whether novices can stop a turn, recover from inversion, keep a target in view and control speed while dodging. Tune camera, input shaping and energy together. Compare level-assist preferences with real players.

3. Make danger readable and fair

Retain the current evade-window cue while evaluating pursuit, missile warnings, target selection and recovery time across difficulties. A failed dodge should teach something. A future outflyable-missile design must model the real throttle ramp and actual reaction/latency, not idealized instantaneous acceleration.

4. Polish mission rhythm

Current missions are richer than the old 22–116 second automated run. Measure their present quiet-to-intense rhythm. Give three showcase sorties distinct decisions, a strong turn and a satisfying ending; avoid padding hold timers to manufacture length.

5. Let scenery support the route

Terrain, districts and uploaded assets are already present. Check approaches, landmark readability, objective altitude, unseen collision, water/road grounding and target contrast. Test the same demanding route on a baseline phone before increasing density.

6. Make quality measurable

Record tutorial drop-off, deaths by cause, failed locks, evade timing, mission duration, retries and frame-time percentiles. Combine these with short interviews: can players explain why they won or lost, and do they choose to replay?

05 / Recommended sequence

Review, tune, then expand.

Use these completion gates rather than speculative dates. Delegate implementation by file ownership, with the orchestrator reviewing evidence and the owner deciding the experience bar.

Accept one exact review build

The fresh build and automated checks below are green. Identify the matching published place version separately, and review audio, first-launch flow, pause/resume and cloud rejoin. Start baseline-phone measurement immediately.

Exit: Owner can identify the build, hear dialogue/SFX, launch and retry a sortie, and preserve progress after rejoining.

Make the first ten minutes intuitive

Use M1/M2 with new touch and controller players. Observe steering corrections, throttle, tutorial recovery, locks, fire and evade without coaching. Tune prompts and camera based on observed confusion.

Exit: Players complete basic actions and explain defensive timing; failures are understandable and retry is quick.

Finish three showcase sorties

Polish a training/intercept, terrain/escort and ace mission—for example M2, M12 and M27. Refine tactical roles, pacing, landmark approaches and audio hierarchy together.

Exit: Each sortie feels different, objectives stay readable, and players voluntarily replay at least one.

Add one high-value replay feature

Prototype adaptive music across transit/combat/ace phases. After the default aircraft feels good, trial a second aircraft with a real handling tradeoff. Tune ranks/challenges using observed player results.

Exit: The addition changes player experience or replay motivation without undermining phone performance or clarity.

Carry the quality bar across the campaign

Audit all 27 missions for pacing, difficulty progression, story/audio continuity, special-weapon usefulness and cleanup. Run integrated engine, full campaign and physical-device acceptance on the same candidate.

Exit: No act relies on repeated chores; saves, audio and performance remain dependable over sustained sessions.

What I would add next: a small adaptive music layer, after published audio is proven. The highest-value immediate work is tuning the systems already merged, especially onboarding, defensive clarity and one memorable ace. Defer a huge aircraft roster, open world, PvP/co-op and an economy until the solo campaign meets its quality bar.

Your first review session

  1. Listen first: confirm opening dialogue, engine and UI sounds on the official game version.
  2. Fly M1: try short inputs, speed changes, a loop and recovery; judge whether the tutorial explains what you need.
  3. Fight M2: track targets, acquire lock, dodge and use a special. Note any hit you cannot explain.
  4. Interrupt and return: pause, adjust settings, resume, retry and rejoin; confirm progress and audio behavior.
  5. Judge a later showcase: review one escort/terrain sortie and an ace, then decide whether the next investment should be feel, pacing, presentation or content.
